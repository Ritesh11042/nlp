SLIDE 2: THE PROBLEM STATEMENT — THE PREPROCESSING DILEMMA
================================================================================
SLIDE TITLE: The Problem: The "One-Size-Fits-All" Preprocessing Dilemma

BULLET POINTS:
• The Standard Industry Habit:
  - Text cleaning is traditionally treated as a rigid, uniform preliminary chore.
  - Universal steps: global lowercasing, stopword removal, and punctuation stripping.
• The Real-World Reality:
  - Modern NLP models process multi-domain text streams simultaneously:
    * Biomedical Literature (PubMed, clinical trial notes)
    * Financial Disclosures (earnings calls, regulatory filings)
    * Social Media & Microblogs (X/Twitter, reviews, chat logs)
• The Fundamental Conflict:
  - What is noise in one domain is critical semantic signal in another!
  - Case-sensitivity distinguishes proteins from genes; numbers dictate financial solvency; emojis express sentiment.

[VISUAL LAYOUT SUGGESTION]:
• 3 columns showing Biomedical text, Financial text, and Social Media text with contrasting characteristics.
• A large red "X" over a single global preprocessing funnel.

[SPEAKER NOTES]:
"In standard NLP pipelines, text preprocessing is treated as a routine chore—practitioners universally lowercase words, strip punctuation, and drop stopwords. But text is no longer homogeneous. In biomedical text, casing separates genes from proteins; in finance, numbers determine whether a company made a profit or loss; in social text, emojis convey the entire sentiment. Applying one uniform cleaning pipeline to all domains is fundamentally flawed."

================================================================================
SLIDE TITLE: Motivation: Empirical Proof of Signal Destruction (Table 1.1)

BULLET POINTS:
• What Happens When You Apply Aggressive Social Cleaning (P1) Globally?
  - 86% of Financial Numbers are Lost: "$12.4M", "18.4%" are wiped out.
  - 100% of Biomedical Case is Lost: "IL-2", "VEGFR2" are lowercased and obscured.
  - 100% of Hyphenated Chemical Entities are Broken: "Sprague-Dawley", "ischemia-reperfusion" are split.
  - Negation Inversion: "not good" becomes "good" when stopwords/punctuations are stripped naively.
• The Trade-off:
  - Minimal cleaning retains too much noise in social feeds.
  - Aggressive cleaning destroys critical domain knowledge.
• Our Question: Can we predict and route the optimal preprocessing policy per text cluster without human labels?

SLIDE 6: THE FOUR INTERPRETABLE PREPROCESSING POLICIES
================================================================================
SLIDE TITLE: The Four Domain-Specific Preprocessing Policies

BULLET POINTS:
• Policy P0: Minimal / Technical Policy
  - Strips URLs; normalizes whitespace.
  - Retains: Case, punctuation, numeric tokens, financial codes.
  - Best for: Financial logs, code-mixed text, technical documentation.
• Policy P1: Aggressive Social Policy
  - Strips @mentions, #hashtags, punctuation, character elongations ("soooo" -> "soo").
  - Lowercases all tokens.
  - Best for: Informal microblogs, Twitter feeds, spam-heavy chat.
• Policy P2: Negation-Shielded Policy
  - Detects negation cues ("not", "never", "cannot").
  - Prefixes affected tokens with 'NOT_' ("NOT_happy", "NOT_acceptable").
  - Best for: Sentiment analysis, customer review classification.
• Policy P3: Biomedical Entity-Preserving Policy
  - Decodes medical emojis into tokens ([EMOJI_DNA], [EMOJI_PILL]).
  - Strictly preserves clinical casing, hyphenated compounds, and dosages.
  - Best for: Electronic Health Records, PubMed abstracts, clinical trials.

[VISUAL LAYOUT SUGGESTION]:
• 2x2 grid representing P0, P1, P2, and P3 with distinctive colored badges matching the Streamlit app.

[SPEAKER NOTES]:
"We formalize four domain-specific policies. P0 is Minimal Technical, keeping numbers and case. P1 is Aggressive Social, cleaning hashtags, mentions, and elongated words. P2 is Negation-Shielded, which attaches a 'NOT_' prefix to prevent sentiment inversion. P3 is Biomedical Entity Preserving, shielding clinical drug names, hyphenated anatomy, and dosages."

SLIDE 7: LABEL-FREE 9-DIMENSIONAL META-PROFILER
================================================================================
SLIDE TITLE: Label-Free 9-D Profiler: Capturing Structural Signatures

BULLET POINTS:
• How do we know what domain a text belongs to without human labels?
• We compute a 9-dimensional statistical meta-vector:
  1. Type-Token Ratio (TTR): Measures lexical diversity (|V| / N).
  2. Uppercase Character Ratio: Detects acronym density vs lowercase chatter.
  3. Digit Density: Identifies quantitative financial data and clinical dosages.
  4. Punctuation Entropy: Shannon entropy of punctuation distributions.
  5. Part-of-Speech (POS) Entropy: Syntactic complexity of sentence structures.
  6. Mean Dependency Depth: Grammatical depth of dependency parse trees.
  7. Out-of-Vocabulary (OOV) Rate: Rare or specialized domain jargon density.
  8. BPE Fragmentation Ratio: Subword tokenizer fragmentations per word.
  9. Embedding Dispersion: Variance of sentence vectors around cluster centroid.

[VISUAL LAYOUT SUGGESTION]:
• Radar / Spider chart showing the distinct 9-D profiles of Biomedical vs Financial vs Social text.

[SPEAKER NOTES]:
"Instead of requiring manual labels, we extract a 9-dimensional statistical meta-vector. For example, financial text has a high digit density; biomedical text has high uppercase ratio and OOV rate; social text has high punctuation entropy. This 9-dimensional vector creates a unique fingerprint that allows our router to make accurate decisions."

SLIDE 11: PRECISION SPELL-CHECKER & DATASET ENTITY SHIELDING
================================================================================
SLIDE TITLE: Precision Spell-Checker & Dataset Entity Shielding

BULLET POINTS:
• Multi-Tier Classification Backed by Dataset Provenance:
  - VALID_STANDARD: Standard English vocabulary (e.g., 'rising', 'profit', 'reported').
  - DOMAIN_ENTITY: Verified terms from BC5CDR and PhraseBank (Shielded from corruption!).
  - MISSPELLED: Verified typos (e.g., 'patinet' -> 'patient', 'operting' -> 'operating').
  - NUMERIC_DATA: Quantitative values and percentages (e.g., '14.5%', 'EUR 12.4M').
• Real Example:
  - Input: "The patinet was admistered high-dose ramucirumab with operting profit rising 14.5%."
  - Output: "The patient was administered high-dose ramucirumab with operating profit rising 14.5%."
  - Result: Typos corrected; 'ramucirumab' (verified in BC5CDR) and 'profit' (verified in PhraseBank) strictly preserved!
